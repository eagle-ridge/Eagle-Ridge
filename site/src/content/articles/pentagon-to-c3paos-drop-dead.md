In 1975 the Daily News summed up a presidential speech to New York in four words: "Ford to City: Drop Dead." Ford never said it. The headline stuck because it was true in every way that mattered.

On September 3, 2026, the Pentagon sent the same message to the CMMC assessment industry. It also never said it out loud.

## What happened

A class deviation signed by John Tenaglia, DOD's principal director for defense pricing, contracting and acquisition policy, went out to contracting officers. It tells them to strip third-party assessment requirements out of contracts. Before this memo, the Phase 2 third-party assessment requirement, due to start this November, was suspended for a 60-day review. A suspension is a memo, and another memo undoes it. A class deviation lives inside the acquisition process, and undoing it takes real work.

The Department of Defense gave its reason in plain language: the program got too bureaucratic and too expensive, and small businesses were paying the most. The July request for information drew more than 1,100 comments. The comments said the same thing.

So the Certified Third-Party Assessment Organizations, the C3PAOs, lost their mandated customer. Their pipeline rested on a rule that no longer exists in contracts.

## What did not happen

Every small defense contractor we talk to read the news as "we're off the hook." Read it again.

You still self-attest to NIST SP 800-171 in SPRS. DOD still audits those attestations. And the False Claims Act still applies to a score you post and cannot prove. Logzone reported a near-perfect score. A DOD audit found it wildly inaccurate, and in June the company paid [$507,144 to settle with the Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/alabama-defense-contractor-agrees-pay-507144-resolve-false-claims-act-liability-relating).

The Pentagon fired the auditor. It kept the lawyer.

## The auditor was the friendly version

A C3PAO assessment had one virtue most people never appreciated. It found your gaps and told you about them before anyone with subpoena power did. You could fail, fix, and come back.

What remains is a Department of Justice attorney who reads your SPRS score after the fact and asks you to prove it. There is no readiness review before that conversation. There is no second attempt.

Proving it is the hard part. An assessor on r/CMMC put documentation at 70 percent of the work. Not documented means not met. Your firewall does not testify. Your System Security Plan does.

## What to do this week

The advice did not change. The urgency did.

1. **Pull your SPRS score today.** If someone typed it in two years ago, it is a liability, not an asset.
2. **Rescore against the DoD assessment methodology.** Work the 3-point and 5-point controls first. A Plan of Action and Milestones only covers 1-point controls, and only if your score already clears the conditional threshold.
3. **Write the policies your SSP references.** An SSP that cites policies that do not exist scores NOT MET on every control those policies cover.
4. **Collect evidence as you go.** Screenshots and log exports dated this quarter. A scramble the week the auditor calls is not evidence.
5. **Keep the documentation current.** Tools drift. The SSP has to drift with them or it becomes fiction, and fiction is what the False Claims Act punishes.

## What comes next

The CMMC Reform Task Force finished its 60-day review on September 11. Its recommendations go to DOD Chief Information Officer Kirsten Davies first, and she decides if and when they become public. Davies told the Billington Cybersecurity Summit this week: "This isn't about whether cybersecurity is important or not. It is. It's critical. It's vital." Anyone telling you what the next version of CMMC looks like is guessing. Do not plan around a guess.

Plan around the two things that survived the deviation: your attestation and your liability.
