In 1975 the Daily News summed up a presidential speech to New York in four words: "Ford to City: Drop Dead." Ford never said it. The headline stuck because it was true in every way that mattered.

On September 3, 2026, the Pentagon sent the same message to the CMMC assessment industry, without ever using the words.

## What happened

Contracting officers got a class deviation telling them to strip third-party assessment requirements out of contracts. John Tenaglia, DOD's principal director for defense pricing, contracting and acquisition policy, signed it.

That changed the standing of the pause. Phase 2 third-party assessments were due to start this November, and DOD had suspended them for a 60-day review. A suspension is one memo, and the next memo can undo it. A class deviation runs through the acquisition process, so reversing it takes real work.

DOD gave its reason plainly: the program had grown too bureaucratic and too expensive, and small businesses were paying the most. The July request for information drew more than 1,100 comments making the same complaint.

The C3PAOs, the Certified Third-Party Assessment Organizations, just lost their mandated customer. Their business rested on a rule that contracts no longer carry.

## What did not happen

Most small defense contractors we talk to read the news as "we're off the hook." Read it again.

You still self-attest to NIST SP 800-171 in SPRS, and DOD still audits those attestations. The False Claims Act still reaches a score you post and cannot prove. Logzone reported a near-perfect score. A DOD audit found it wildly inaccurate, and in June the company paid [$507,144 to settle with the Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/alabama-defense-contractor-agrees-pay-507144-resolve-false-claims-act-liability-relating).

The Pentagon fired the auditor. It kept the lawyer.

## The auditor was the friendly version

A C3PAO assessment did one thing few people ever thanked it for. It found your gaps and told you about them before anyone with subpoena power did. You could fail, fix, and come back.

Now a Justice Department attorney reads your SPRS score after the fact and asks you to prove it. Nobody reviews your readiness first, and you get one attempt.

Proving it is the hard part. An assessor on r/CMMC put documentation at 70 percent of the work. Not documented means not met. Your firewall does not testify. Your System Security Plan does.

## What to do this week

Same work as last month, on a shorter clock.

1. **Pull your SPRS score today.** If someone typed it in two years ago, treat it as a liability until you can prove otherwise.
2. **Rescore against the DoD assessment methodology.** Work the 3-point and 5-point controls first. A Plan of Action and Milestones only covers 1-point controls, and only if your score already clears the conditional threshold.
3. **Write the policies your SSP references.** An SSP that cites policies that do not exist scores NOT MET on every control those policies cover.
4. **Collect evidence as you go.** Screenshots and log exports dated this quarter. A scramble the week the auditor calls comes too late to count.
5. **Keep the documentation current.** Tools drift. Let the SSP drift with them or it turns into fiction, and fiction is what the False Claims Act punishes.

## What comes next

The CMMC Reform Task Force finished its 60-day review on September 11. Its recommendations go to DOD Chief Information Officer Kirsten Davies first, and she decides if and when they become public. Davies told the Billington Cybersecurity Summit this week: "This isn't about whether cybersecurity is important or not. It is. It's critical. It's vital."

Anyone who tells you what the next version of CMMC looks like is guessing. Plan around the two things that survived the deviation instead: your attestation and your liability.
