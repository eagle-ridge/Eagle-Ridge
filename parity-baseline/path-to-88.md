# The path to 88

Every defense contractor handling CUI is required to have an SPRS score. It runs
from **110** down to **−203**, and a conditional
CMMC certification requires at least **88**.
Score yourself honestly for the first time and you will probably land far
below it — often below zero.

This page shows what an eight-week Eagle Ridge engagement does to that
number, using a representative eight-person machine shop.

Fig. 01 · The scale you are scored on

The DoD scores all 110 NIST 800-171 controls. Each gap subtracts
1, 3, or 5 points. Miss every one and you lose 313 — from 110 all the way
down to −203.

## One engagement, one number

Everything in the engagement moves one number. First we find out where
you actually stand — no sugarcoating, because the assessor won’t
sugarcoat either. Then the rest of the engagement climbs the score back over
the line, with the few small items left over on a formal remediation plan the
rule recognizes (a POA&M).

Fig. 02 · A representative engagement, start to submission

Representative eight-person shop: seventeen 5-point gaps, six
3-point, nineteen 1-point. Weights come from the official DoD Assessment
Methodology; the submitted score keeps 19 points of one-point items on a
POA&M, under the 22 points the 88 threshold leaves you.

## All 110 controls, accounted for

An assessor marks each control met or not met — only two controls
(MFA and FIPS encryption) carry partial credit. We assess all 110 in the
first two weeks, fix what moves the score, and put the rest on the
POA&M.

Fig. 03 · Where the 110 controls stand

Hatched items are open but accepted: a POA&M is the DoD’s
formal way to let you certify now and close the small gaps inside 180 days.
Close them and the certification goes Final; miss the 180 days and it lapses.
Most one-point controls qualify, a few are excluded outright, and one
five-point control qualifies only at partial credit — we verify
eligibility item by item.

## Eight weeks, start to assessor

The work overlaps on purpose. While you and your IT support close technical
gaps, we write the System Security Plan — the document the assessor
reads first. Then we review it the way an assessor will, and keep reviewing
until there are no critical findings left.

Fig. 04 · The engagement, week by week

Remediation (weeks 2–4) is the heaviest lift; the dashed bar at
the end is the assessment itself, run by an independent certified
assessor — a C3PAO. The seven weeks before it exist to make that week
boring.

## Your hours vs. ours

You run a shop, not a security team. The engagement keeps your part short
and specific. We do the document-heavy work.

### What you do

* Answer the intake questionnaire and one structured call
* Gather the evidence we list — screenshots, invoices, settings
* Approve tool purchases and let IT apply the config changes
* Sign policies and sit for a practice interview

### What Eagle Ridge does

* Assess all 110 controls and score you the way the DoD will
* Prioritize fixes by points-per-dollar for a small-shop budget
* Draft the SSP’s 110 control narratives and the POA&M
* Review it assessor-style, package evidence, prep your people

Figures show a representative engagement, not a quote — your
starting score and timeline depend on your systems. Scoring follows the DoD
Assessment Methodology; a Registered Provider reviews every deliverable before
it reaches you. The SSP is delivered gap-documented and becomes
assessment-ready once policies are signed and tools are deployed.
Sources: DoD NIST SP 800-171 Assessment Methodology v1.2.1 (2020) · 32 CFR
Part 170 (CMMC Program, §170.21) · NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2.
